General crop advisory principles

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) combines field monitoring, cultural practices, mechanical controls, biological controls and need-based chemical controls. Farmers should regularly inspect the field, identify the pest correctly, observe whether damage is increasing and seek local expert guidance before selecting a pesticide.

Rice crop monitoring

For rice, inspect plants regularly for changes in leaf colour, unusual spots, stem damage, dead hearts, white ears and insect presence. Record the crop growth stage and the affected area. Avoid making a diagnosis from one symptom alone because nutrient deficiencies, water stress, disease and pests can produce similar symptoms. Maintain suitable field drainage and follow locally recommended nutrient management. Consult the nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) or agriculture department for locally validated recommendations.

Soil health

Soil testing helps determine soil pH and nutrient status. Use a soil test from an authorized soil testing laboratory before making major fertilizer changes. Add organic matter such as well-decomposed compost where suitable. Avoid applying fertilizer based only on leaf colour because several factors can cause yellowing.

Water management

Irrigation frequency depends on crop, soil texture, crop stage, rainfall and local water availability. Sandy soils generally drain faster than clay soils. Check soil moisture and local agricultural recommendations rather than following a fixed schedule for every farm. Avoid waterlogging in crops that are sensitive to prolonged standing water.

Safe pesticide practices

Correct pest identification is essential before choosing a pesticide. Use only products registered for the crop and target pest, and follow the product label and current government recommendations. Wear the protective equipment specified on the label, observe the re-entry interval and pre-harvest interval, and keep chemicals away from children, animals, food and water sources. Never mix products unless the label explicitly permits it. Do not use an AI-generated dosage as a substitute for the product label or expert advice.

Yellowing leaves: possible causes

Yellow leaves can be associated with nutrient deficiency, waterlogging, drought, root damage, pests, disease or natural leaf ageing. Check whether yellowing occurs on older or younger leaves, whether it is uniform or patchy, whether roots are healthy, and whether the field is too wet or dry. Photograph affected plants and note recent fertilizer and irrigation events. A soil or plant diagnosis may be needed. Seek local expert advice before applying fertilizer or pesticide.
